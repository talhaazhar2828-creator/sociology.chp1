1st Topic: Introduction to Sociology

1.1 Meaning and origin of the word

The word Sociology is built from two parts: the Latin socius (companion, society) and the Greek logos (study, science). So it literally means "the study of society". The term was introduced by Auguste Comte in 1838.

1.2 Definition from your notes

Sociology is the scientific study of social life, society, human life and culture, social interaction, social behaviour and social institutions.

Word by word:

  • Scientific study – it uses observation, surveys, interviews and data, not just opinion.
  • Social life / society – the organised web of relationships among people living together.
  • Culture – shared beliefs, language, customs, values, food, dress and art of a group.
  • Social interaction – how people act toward and react to each other (talking, cooperating, competing).
  • Social behaviour – behaviour shaped by the presence and expectations of other people.
  • Social institutions – lasting structures such as family, religion, education, government and economy.

1.4 Auguste Comte (1798–1857)

  • French philosopher, called the Father / Founder of Sociology; coined the word in 1838 (he first used the name "social physics").
  • Main book: Course of Positive Philosophy (Cours de philosophie positive).
  • Positivism: knowledge should come from observation and evidence, not guesswork.
  • Law of Three Stages of human thought: (1) Theological – events explained by gods and spirits; (2) Metaphysical – explained by abstract forces; (3) Positive/Scientific – explained by observation and laws.
  • Social Statics = study of social order and stability (family, institutions). Social Dynamics = study of social change and progress.
  • He ranked sociology as the "queen of sciences", the last and most complex science to develop.

1.5 Ibn Khaldun (1332–1406) and Imraniyat

  • Muslim scholar, historian and thinker born in Tunis; regarded by many as a pioneer / forerunner of sociology, about 450 years before Comte.
  • His famous work is the Muqaddimah (Introduction to History, 1377).
  • He named his new science 'Ilm al-'Umran (Imraniyat) – the science of human civilisation and social organisation. 'Umran means settled life, population and civilisation.
  • Asabiyyah (group feeling / social solidarity): the bond that holds a group together and gives it power.
  • Studied nomadic (Bedouin) versus settled (urban) life, and the rise, growth and decline of states in cycles, explaining them by social and economic causes rather than luck or fate.

1.6 "Man is a social animal"

A famous statement (linked to the Greek philosopher Aristotle) meaning humans cannot live or develop alone. A child learns language, manners and values only from other people, and needs family, food, education and safety that only society can provide. This is why studying society matters.

1.7 Nature and characteristics of sociology

  • It is a social science, not a natural science.
  • It is empirical (based on observation) and rational (based on reasoning).
  • It is general: it studies patterns in groups, not one individual.
  • It is both theoretical (builds concepts) and applied (solves problems).
  • It is objective: it describes what is, not what ought to be.

2nd Topic: Subject Matter of Sociology

"Subject matter" means what sociology studies. The ten points from your notes are explained below.

1. Gender Study

Studies the social roles, values, rights, limits and needs of men and women, and how society treats them. Sex is biological, gender is socially created. Topics: gender roles, equality, discrimination, women's education and work, harassment.

2. Social Work

A helping profession that uses sociological knowledge to help individuals, families and communities solve problems. Works with poverty, disability, orphans, addiction and family conflict through counselling, case work, group work and referral to services.

3. Criminology

Studies why crimes happen, their causes (poverty, unemployment, bad company, broken families), the criminal's behaviour, punishment and how to prevent crime and reform offenders.

4. Community Development

A group of people working together to solve shared problems and improve their own living conditions, such as clean water, sanitation, roads, schools and health. Key idea: participation – the community itself takes part in planning and action.

5. Civics and Community Engagement

Citizens' rights and duties: voting, paying taxes, obeying laws, respecting others, protecting public property. Community engagement means active involvement in local matters such as volunteering and cleanliness drives.

6. Sociology of Religion

Studies how religion shapes society: values, rules, festivals, family life, morality and social unity. It studies religion as a social institution and how society and religion affect each other. It does not judge which religion is true.

7. Sociology of Education

Studies how social forces (class, gender, income, culture) affect education, and how education affects society. Topics: school as an institution, dropout rates, access to schooling, curriculum, teacher–student roles. Job: Lecturer

8. Socialization extra detail: not in your notes

The lifelong process by which a person learns the norms, values, language and behaviour of their society and becomes a member of it.
  • Agents: family (most important), peer group, school, religion, mass media, workplace.
  • Primary socialization: in childhood, mainly at home. Secondary: later, through school and work.
  • Thinkers: C.H. Cooley – looking-glass self (we see ourselves as others see us); G.H. Mead – learning roles by taking the role of others.

9. Research Methodology

The systematic way sociologists collect and analyse information about society.

Steps from your notes: Survey → Interview → Data collection → Analyze → Results / Conclusion

  • Survey: asking the same questions to many people, e.g. 200 people.
  • Interview: face-to-face talk about a problem.
  • Data collection: gathering the information obtained.
  • Analyze: counting and studying data (how many said yes or no).
  • Results / Conclusion: final findings from the whole process.

10. Project Planning and Management extra detail: not in your notes

Planning and running social projects (for example a literacy programme) step by step:
  • Identify the problem and assess needs → set objectives → make a plan and budget → arrange resources and staff → implement → monitor progress → evaluate results → report.
  • Sociology helps by understanding the community's real needs and culture before the project begins.

4th Topic: Sociology as a Science

Science means observation and collection of data. Science is of two kinds: natural (physics, chemistry, biology) and social (sociology, economics, psychology, political science).

Why sociology is called a science

  • Empirical: uses observation, surveys and interviews, not only imagination.
  • Follows the research cycle: survey → interview → data collection → analysis → results.
  • Objective: aims to study facts without personal bias.
  • Uses theory and testing: ideas are checked against evidence. Durkheim's study of suicide (1897) used statistics to show social causes.
  • Cause and effect: looks for patterns and causes; results can be checked by others.
  • Predictive to a degree: can forecast trends such as population growth.

Natural science versus sociology

PointNatural scienceSociology
SubjectMatter, natureHuman behaviour and society
ExperimentsControlled laboratory testsDifficult; uses surveys and observation
ExactnessVery exact lawsLess exact; humans change and have choices
ObjectivityEasierHarder; researcher is part of society

Conclusion: Sociology is a social science. It is not as exact as physics, but it uses scientific methods, so it is rightly called a science.

5th Topic: Background and History of Sociology

Your notes only list "Comte (1838)" and "Ibn Khaldun (Imraniyat)". Add today's lecture points here. The general background is below.

Causes that led to sociology

  • French Revolution (1789): disturbed the old social order; thinkers wanted to understand social change.
  • Industrial Revolution (18th–19th century): factories, cities, migration, poverty, new social problems.
  • Growth of natural science: encouraged studying society scientifically.
  • Enlightenment: emphasis on reason and evidence over tradition.

Quick Revision Q&A

Q. Who coined the term sociology and when?
Auguste Comte, 1838.
Q. What did Ibn Khaldun call his science?
'Ilm al-'Umran (Imraniyat), the science of civilisation and society.
Q. Name the steps of the research method.
Survey, interview, data collection, analysis, results/conclusion.
Q. Difference between qualitative and quantitative?
Qualitative uses words and opinions; quantitative uses numbers and counts.
Q. Why is sociology a science?
It uses observation, data collection and analysis, and studies society by evidence.
Q. Name four scopes of sociology.
Social Welfare Department, Health Care Center, Old Age Home, Child Protection Bureau (or any from the 11).
